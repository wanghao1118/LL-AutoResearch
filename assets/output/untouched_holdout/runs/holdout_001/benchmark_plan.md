### Introduction

General autonomous agents capable of reasoning and decision-making in a variety of environments have been of longstanding interest in the field of artificial intelligence. While this has traditionally been studied in reinforcement learning, the recent rise of language models (LMs) with strong reasoning and general adaptability offers an alternative paradigm. Not only have LMs excelled in standard natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as summarization and language inference , but they have also been adapted to an increasingly diverse set of tasks that often require advanced common-sense reasoning or quantitative skills . Despite such strengths, these methods are reflexive and fall short of humans' deliberate and thoughtful decision-making characteristics to solve problems . In particular, they fail to consider multiple reasoning paths or to plan ahead. Recent search-guided LM work addresses this issue by searching over multiple reasoning chains.
